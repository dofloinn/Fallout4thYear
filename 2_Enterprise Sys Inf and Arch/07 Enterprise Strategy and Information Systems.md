### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Strategic IT</mark> 

Technology is no longer an afterthought in business strategy, but the cause and driver  

IT can change the way businesses compete  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">A strategic information system is any information system that uses IT to help an organisation:</mark>  
- Gain a competitive advantage  
- Reduce a competitive disadvantage  
- Or meet other strategic enterprise objectives

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Does IT matter?</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">No ?</mark>  Nicholas Carr argued that IT is infrastructure, much like electricity, too commonplace to offer a competitive advantage  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Yes ?</mark> It is not just networks and computers. The important part is the software and information and how IT is used

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Competitive Forces</mark>

To succeed, a business must develop strategies to counter these forces...  
- Rivalry of competitors within its industry
- New entrants into an industry and its markets  
- Substitute products that may capture market share  
- Bargaining power of customers  
- Bargaining power of suppliers

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Five Competitive Strategies</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cost Leadership:</mark> 
- Become low-cost producers  
- Help suppliers or customers reduce costs  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Differentiation Strategy:</mark>
- Differentiate a company’s products from its competitors’
- Focus on a particular segment or niche of market  
- Example: IKEA has a 3D room designer feature  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Innovation Strategy:</mark>
- Unique products, services, or markets  
- Radical changes to business processes  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Growth Strategy:</mark>
- Expand company’s capacity to produce
- Expand into global markets  
- Diversify into new products or services  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Alliance Strategy:</mark>
- Establish linkages and alliances with customers, suppliers, competitors, consultants, and other companies 
- Includes mergers, acquisitions, joint ventures, virtual companies  
- Example: Wal-Mart uses automatic inventory replenishment by supplier

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Using Competitive Strategies</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">These strategies are not mutually exclusive:</mark>
- Organisations use one, some, or all 
- A given activity could fall into one or more categories of competitive strategy

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Other Competitive Strategies</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Lock in Customers and Suppliers:</mark> Deter them from switching to competitors  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Build in Switching Costs:</mark>
- Make customers and suppliers dependent on the use of innovative IS  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Erect Barriers to Entry:</mark>
- Discourage or delay other companies from entering the market 
- Increase the technology or investment needed to enter  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Build Strategic IT Capabilities:</mark>
- Take advantage of strategic opportunities when they arise
- Improve efficiency of business practices  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Leverage Investment in IT:</mark> Develop products and service that would not be possible without a strong IT capability

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Business Process Reengineering</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Called BPR or simply Reengineering:</mark>
- Fundamental rethinking and radical redesign of business processes  
- Seeks to achieve improvements in cost, quality, speed, and service  

Potential <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">payback</mark> is high, but so is <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">risk</mark> of disruption and failure  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Organisational redesign</mark> approaches are an important enabler of reengineering. Includes use of IT, process teams, case managers

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Creating a Virtual Company</mark>

A Virtual Company is an organisation that operates primarily through the internet, bypassing the need for a central physical headquarters. It leverages technology to unite a distributed workforce, allowing the business to remain agile, global, and cost-effective.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">A virtual company uses IT to link:</mark>
- People  
- Organisations
- Assets  
- Ideas  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Inter-enterprise information systems link:</mark>
- Customers
- Suppliers
- Subcontractors
- Competitors

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Virtual Companies</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Core Characteristics:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Location Independent:</mark> Employees work from anywhere in the world - home offices, co-working spaces...  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Tech-Driven:</mark> Operations rely on cloud computing, and project management tools.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Fluid Structure:</mark> Often uses a mix of full-time remote staff and specialised freelancers.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Reduced Overhead:</mark> Minimal costs spent on rent, utilities, and office supplies.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">GitLab:</mark> One of the world's largest all-remote organisations with over 2,000 employees in 60+ countries and no physical offices.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Zapier:</mark> A pioneer in remote work, they have been 100% distributed since their foundation in 2011.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Buffer:</mark> Fully remote since 2015.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Airbnb:</mark> While they have offices, they transitioned to a "Live and Work Anywhere" policy, operating as a hybrid-virtual giant.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Building a Knowledge-Creating Company</mark>

A knowledge-creating company or learning organisation:  
- Consistently creates new business knowledge  
- Disseminates it throughout the company  
- Builds it into its products and services  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Two kinds of knowledge:</mark>  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Explicit Knowledge</mark> 
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Tacit Knowledge</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Explicit Knowledge:</mark> Data, documents, and things written down or stored in computers

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Tacit Knowledge:</mark> 
- The “how-to” knowledge in workers’ minds  
- Represents some of the most important information within an organisation  
- A knowledge-creating company makes tacit knowledge available to others

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Knowledge Management</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Successful knowledge management:</mark>
- Creates techniques, technologies, systems, and rewards for getting employees to share what they know
- Makes better use of accumulated workplace and enterprise knowledge

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Enterprise Intelligence:</mark>
- Leveraging organisational "know-how"  
- Performance support  
- Interacting with operational databases  
- Building expert networks  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Information Creation, Sharing & Management:</mark>
- Capturing & distributing expert stories  
- Real-time information management  
- Communication and collaboration  
- New content creation  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Document Management:</mark> Use of online document management systems

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Knowledge Management Systems (KMS)</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Knowledge management systems:</mark> 
- A major strategic use of IT
- Manages organisational learning and know-how 
- Helps knowledge workers create, organise, and make available important knowledge
- Makes this knowledge available wherever and whenever it is needed  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Knowledge includes:</mark> Processes, procedures, patents, reference works, formulas, best practices, forecasts, and fixes
