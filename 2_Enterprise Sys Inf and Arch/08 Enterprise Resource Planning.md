### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP: Enterprise Resource Planning</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP</mark> is a cross-functional enterprise backbone that integrates and automates processes within  
- Manufacturing  
- Logistics / Distribution  
- Finance / Accounting  
- Human resources  
- and more...

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP - Definitions</mark>  

A process of managing all resources and their use in the entire enterprise in a coordinated manner - ERP software ties all departments in a company together into one common system  

ERP is a set of integrated business applications, or modules which carry out common business functions such as general ledger, sales forecasting....  

Support business through optimizing, maintaining, and tracking business functions: HR, financial, manufacturing etc...  

Therefore providing consistent information for timely decision-making and performance measurement

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Enterprise Resource Planning</mark> 

A process of <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">managing</mark> all <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">resources</mark> and their use in the entire enterprise in a coordinated manner  

ERP is a <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">set of integrated business applications</mark>, or modules which carry out common business functions such as general ledger, sales forecasting....  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Support</mark> business through <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">optimising, maintaining, and tracking</mark> business functions such as HR, financial, manufacturing etc...  

Focused on <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">value chains</mark>, rather than individual functions  

More about <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">business process change</mark> than technology  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP core capabilities include:</mark>  
- Integrated modules
- Common process and data models and definitions  
- Common database - update one module, automatically updates others

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Benefits of ERP</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Common set of data:</mark> Removes consistency and synchronisation issues  

Library of available <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">standard template processes</mark> and modules make integration easier

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Inter-department integration for all departments using the ERP:</mark>
- Allow companies to <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">better understand</mark> their business.  
- Helps companies <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">standardise business processes</mark> and more easily enact best practices.  
- More <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">efficient processes</mark> enable companies to concentrate their efforts on serving their customers, maximizing profit, and building a competitive advantage.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Potential Limitations of ERP</mark>  

Global ERP can be a <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">never-ending project</mark> for large organisations  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">No organisation exists in isolation - value chain business processes:</mark>  
- There are always suppliers and clients who use <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">different data models</mark>.
- This means that the <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">need for integration</mark> cannot be removed.  

Inter-department integration relies on using the <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">global ERP</mark>. Causes <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">problems</mark> with anomalous departments, recently required, geographically isolated or with different business processes.  

The <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">software can drive the business</mark> rather than the other way around. 

Templates tend to <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">impose the standard business process</mark> rather than your organisation’s business process. This is okay for commoditised processes but not for all.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">The “modular” ERP System</mark>  

Most <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">systems are modular</mark> to <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">permit automating some functions</mark> but not others.  

Some <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">common modules</mark>, such as finance and accounting, are adopted by nearly all users; others such as human resource management are not.  

For example, a <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">service company probably has no need for a manufacturing</mark> module.  

Other companies <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">may already have a system</mark> that they believe to be adequate. Generally speaking, the <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">greater the number of modules selected, the greater the integration benefits</mark>, but also the greater the costs, risks and changes involved.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Elements of ERP</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Decision making modules:</mark> Technology to <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">integrate people</mark>, information and business processes across technologies; e.g. information integration in a data warehouse  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Operations:</mark> All <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">value chain processes</mark> - purchasing, production, sales...

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Finance module</mark>  

The financial module is the core of many <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP software systems</mark>.  

All kind of organisations (small scale, large scale) benefit from the implementation of an ERP financial module.  

It can gather <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">financial data from various functional departments</mark>, and generates valuable financial reports such as:  
- general ledger / balance sheet 
- quarterly financial statements

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Human Resource module</mark> 

Human Resources is another <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">widely implemented ERP module</mark>.  

HR <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">module streamlines the management of human resources</mark> and human capitals. HR modules routinely maintain a complete employee database including:  
- contact information
- salary details
- attendance
- performance evaluation 
- and promotion of all employees.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Purchasing module</mark>  

Purchase module <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">streamlines procurement</mark> of required raw materials.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">It automates the processes of:</mark>
- identifying potential suppliers
- negotiating price
- awarding purchase order to the supplier
- billing processes  

Purchase module is <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">tightly integrated</mark> with the inventory control and production planning modules  

The purchasing module is often <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">integrated with supply chain management</mark> software (see next lecture).

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Sales and Marketing module</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Sales module implements functions:</mark>
- order placement 
- order scheduling  
- shipping and invoicing (in conjunction with other modules).  

Sales module is closely integrated with organisations' <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ecommerce websites</mark>.  

Many ERP vendors offer online store front as part of the sales module.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP marketing module along with Customer relations management (CRM) software (see next lecture) supports:</mark> 
- lead generation  
- direct mailing campaign and other marketing works 
-  scheduling of the promotions

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP Rollout Approaches – Big Bang</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">The entire ERP system goes live in all departments, locations, and processes on a single date.:</mark>  
- All legacy systems are switched off at once.  
- All modules (Finance, HR, Supply Chain, Manufacturing, etc.) go live simultaneously.  
- Requires very intense preparation and testing.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Advantages:</mark> 
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Fast transformation:</mark> shorter overall project timeline.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Single transition:</mark> avoids running old and new systems in parallel.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Immediate integration:</mark> real-time data available across the organisation from day one.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Risks:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">High risk of failure:</mark> any major issue impacts the whole organisation.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Steep learning curve:</mark> staff must adapt quickly.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Limited buffer:</mark> no gradual feedback cycles.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Suitability:</mark>
- Medium-sized organisations.  
- When processes are standardised across all departments.  
- When the cost of parallel systems is too high.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP Rollout Approaches – Phased</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Modules, business units, or geographic locations go live step by step, not all at once:</mark>  
- By Function / Module e.g., Finance → Procurement → HR → Manufacturing.  
- By Geography e.g., Ireland → UK → Europe → Global.  
- By Business Unit e.g., Group of stores, divisions, product lines.  
- By Process e.g., Order-to-Cash first, then Procure-to-Pay.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Advantages:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Lower risk:</mark> impact isolated to one area at a time.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Simplified change management:</mark> staff can be trained gradually.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Early feedback:</mark> issues discovered in early phases can be fixed before later rollouts.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Risks:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Longer timeline:</mark> full deployment takes more time.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Temporary integrations:</mark> may need interfaces between legacy and new systems.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Inconsistency:</mark> different teams may temporarily use different processes.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Suitability:</mark>
- Large or global organisations.  
- Organisations with diverse business units.  
- Situations with high risk if operations fail (e.g., hospitals, logistics).

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP Rollout Approaches – Parallel</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Both the old system and the new ERP run together for a defined period:</mark>  
- Users enter or verify transactions in both systems.  
- Organisation evaluates accuracy and performance of the new ERP before fully switching.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Advantages:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Very low risk:</mark> if ERP errors occur, the legacy system acts as a backup.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Helps user training:</mark> staff can compare systems side-by-side.  
- Gradual confidence-building.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Risks:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Double work:</mark> users must duplicate entries.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Costly:</mark> requires supporting two systems at once.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Delays:</mark> mistakes or mismatches can slow progress.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Suitability:</mark>
- Finance modules requiring high accuracy (e.g., payroll, general ledger).  
- Highly regulated industries.  
- Organisations with strict audit or compliance requirements.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP Rollout Approaches – Pilot</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Rollout begins with a small group, department, or location to test the ERP before organisation-wide deployment:</mark> 
- One plant or team goes live first.  
- They become the “pilot site.”  
- Lessons learned are applied to future rollouts.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Advantages:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Controlled testing:</mark> real-world environment without full exposure.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Feedback loop:</mark> configuration and training improved before expansion.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">User champions:</mark> pilot users can train others.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Risks:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Slow scale-up:</mark> initial pilot phase can take longer.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Non-representative pilot:</mark> if pilot group is unusual, future issues may still arise.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Pilot fatigue:</mark> pilot team may become overburdened.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Suitability:</mark>
- Organisations with varied operations.  
- Projects introducing major process redesign.  
- When executive teams want early proof of value

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Deployment Model and Ownership</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>
- Cloud ERP systems are hosted and maintained by the vendor in their own data centres.  
- Organisations access the system over the internet using a subscription model (usually monthly or annual).  

The <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">vendor</mark> manages the hardware, storage, networking, security, updates, and system performance.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark> On-premise ERP systems are installed on the organisation’s own servers and operated within their own IT environment.  

The <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">organisation</mark> owns and maintains everything: hardware, software, upgrades, and security.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cost Structure</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- Operates on an <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">operational expenditure model (OPEX)</mark>.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">No upfront investment</mark> in servers or infrastructure.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Subscription</mark> includes maintenance, updates, and support.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Lower initial cost</mark> but continuous recurring fees.  

Best suited for organisations wanting predictable long-term costs and minimal IT overhead.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- Operates on a <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">capital expenditure model (CAPEX)</mark>.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Significant upfront investment</mark> in hardware and licenses.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Ongoing costs</mark> include IT staff, maintenance, security, and upgrades.  

Useful where long-term ownership and control outweigh initial cost concerns.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customisation & Flexibility</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- Typically offers <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">standardised functionality</mark> with configuration rather than heavy customisation.  
- Updates may limit highly bespoke changes, as the <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">vendor controls the codebase</mark>.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Faster deployment</mark> because of pre-built workflows.  

Strong choice for organisations willing to adopt industry best-practice processes.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- Allows for <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">extensive customisation</mark>, including modifications to source code.  
- Organisations can <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">tailor workflows</mark> down to the smallest detail.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">More complex</mark> implementations and longer timelines.  

Better for unique or highly specialised business processes.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Security and Data Control</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Vendors invest heavily</mark> in cyber-security, often exceeding what individual organisations can achieve alone.  
- Data is stored externally, which can raise <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">compliance or sovereignty concerns</mark>.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Security responsibilities</mark> follow the shared responsibility model: the vendor protects the infrastructure, and the client controls user access and data practices.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Full control</mark> over data storage, access, and security policies.  
- Favoured in <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">industries with strict compliance</mark> (defence, healthcare, government).  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Greater responsibility:</mark> the organisation must handle backups, patching, and monitoring.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Scalability and Performance</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Highly scalable:</mark> organisations can add users, storage, or features instantly.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Ideal for growing businesses</mark>, multi-site organisations, or global expansions.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Performance is dependent</mark> on internet connectivity and vendor infrastructure.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Scaling requires new servers</mark>, hardware upgrades, and potential downtime.  
- Offers <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">predictable performance</mark> in environments with stable, internal networks.  
- Best suited for <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">organisations with steady workloads</mark> and predictable size.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Upgrades and Innovation</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- Automatic, frequent updates delivered by the vendor.  
- New features, AI enhancements, and security patches arrive continuously.  
- Reduces risk of systems becoming outdated.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- Upgrades must be planned, budgeted for, and executed manually.  
- Organisations may delay upgrades due to cost, leading to “ERP version stagnation”.  
- Highly customised systems face upgrade complications

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Implementation Speed</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- Much faster to implement because infrastructure is already in place.  
- Preconfigured industry templates speed up deployment.  
- Often paired with hybrid methodologies.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- Longer timelines due to hardware setup, customisation, and testing.  
- Typically suited to structured methodologies (e.g. Waterfall)

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Suitability</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- Small-to-medium organisations  
- Rapidly growing companies  
- Businesses adopting standardised processes  
- Companies without large IT departments  
- Distributed teams needing remote access  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- Highly regulated industries  
- Organisations needing extreme customisation  
- Businesses requiring tight control over data  
- Legacy-heavy environments with complex integrations

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP Implementation Risk Factors</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Implementation Risk Factors:</mark>
- No clear goal
- Lack of clear planning
- Under-estimating resources required
- Customisations 
- Insufficient testing

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">No Clear Goal</mark>

Often, <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">lack of consensus</mark> on the problems being solved, the outcome desired, or the specific financial justification of the project, leads to challenges later controlling the scope and maintaining executive sponsorship.  

Having a clear destination means defining the important business processes and financial benefits

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Lack of Detailed Planning</mark>  

All projects of this size start with some kind of plan. However, more times than not, the plan is not realistic, detailed, or specific enough.  

To be a good plan, it needs to identify all the requirements and the people who are going to work on them.  

It needs to be at a level of detail where a knowledgeable person can visualise the work, usually in work blocks of a few days.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Under-Estimating Resources Required</mark>

Having a solid understanding of the internal and external resources needed to complete the project is critical. E.g.:  

For internal resources, understanding the time commitment needed from business users, typically in the Finance, Accounting, or Human Resources departments, is one of the most commonly underestimated areas.  

For external resources, having an agreement up-front with your consultants and contractors about the specific duration, skills, and quantity of resources needed is critical.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customisations</mark>

Customisations, along with interfaces and data conversion, are the main areas of technical risk in ERP implementations.  

Customisations always start out small, but incrementally grow to become the technical challenges that derail these projects.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Insufficient testing</mark>

When schedules get tight, reducing the number and depth of test cycles is often one of the first areas that often gets cut.  

The purpose of testing in an ERP project is not to see if the software works. The purpose is to see if the system meets your business needs and produces the output you need.  

Reducing testing may not leave defects undiscovered, but it certainly increases the risk the ERP system will be missing important functions or not be well accepted by end users
