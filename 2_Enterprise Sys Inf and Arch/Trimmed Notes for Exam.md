### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Data Versus Information</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Data</mark> are raw facts about physical phenomena or business transactions  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Information</mark> is data that has been converted into meaningful and useful context for end users  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Examples:</mark>
- Sales data is names, quantities, and euro amounts
- Sales information is amount of sales by product type, sales territory, or salesperson

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Recognising Information Systems</mark>

Business professionals should be able to look at an information system and identify...  

The people, hardware, software, data, and network resources they use  

The type of information products they produce  

The way they perform input, processing, output, storage, and control activities

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">URI and URL</mark>

A physical resource is a resource one can access and work with such as a file, a Web page, or an e-mail address. A URL is one type of URI.  

An abstract resource is one that doesn’t have any physical existence, the URI is used as an identifier or an ID.  

The URI http://uhosp/patients/ns is simply a text identifier.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Five Competitive Strategies</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cost Leadership:</mark> 
- Become low-cost producers  
- Help suppliers or customers reduce costs  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Differentiation Strategy:</mark>
- Differentiate a company’s products from its competitors’
- Focus on a particular segment or niche of market  
- Example: IKEA has a 3D room designer feature  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Innovation Strategy:</mark>
- Unique products, services, or markets  
- Radical changes to business processes  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Growth Strategy:</mark>
- Expand company’s capacity to produce
- Expand into global markets  
- Diversify into new products or services  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Alliance Strategy:</mark>
- Establish linkages and alliances with customers, suppliers, competitors, consultants, and other companies 
- Includes mergers, acquisitions, joint ventures, virtual companies  
- Example: Wal-Mart uses automatic inventory replenishment by supplier

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Using Competitive Strategies</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">These strategies are not mutually exclusive:</mark>
- Organisations use one, some, or all 
- A given activity could fall into one or more categories of competitive strategy

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Creating a Virtual Company</mark>

A Virtual Company is an organisation that operates primarily through the internet, bypassing the need for a central physical headquarters. It leverages technology to unite a distributed workforce, allowing the business to remain agile, global, and cost-effective.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">A virtual company uses IT to link:</mark>
- People
- Organisations
- Assets
- Ideas

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Inter-enterprise information systems link:</mark>
- Customers
- Suppliers
- Subcontractors
- Competitors

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Virtual Companies</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Core Characteristics:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Location Independent:</mark> Employees work from anywhere in the world - home offices, co-working spaces...  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Tech-Driven:</mark> Operations rely on cloud computing, and project management tools.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Fluid Structure:</mark> Often uses a mix of full-time remote staff and specialised freelancers.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Reduced Overhead:</mark> Minimal costs spent on rent, utilities, and office supplies.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">GitLab:</mark> One of the world's largest all-remote organisations with over 2,000 employees in 60+ countries and no physical offices.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Zapier:</mark> A pioneer in remote work, they have been 100% distributed since their foundation in 2011.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Buffer:</mark> Fully remote since 2015.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Airbnb:</mark> While they have offices, they transitioned to a "Live and Work Anywhere" policy, operating as a hybrid-virtual giant.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Potential Limitations of ERP</mark>  

Global ERP can be a <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">never-ending project</mark> for large organisations  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">No organisation exists in isolation - value chain business processes:</mark>  
- There are always suppliers and clients who use <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">different data models</mark>.
- This means that the <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">need for integration</mark> cannot be removed.  

Inter-department integration relies on using the <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">global ERP</mark>. Causes <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">problems</mark> with anomalous departments, recently required, geographically isolated or with different business processes.  

The <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">software can drive the business</mark> rather than the other way around. 

Templates tend to <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">impose the standard business process</mark> rather than your organisation’s business process. This is okay for commoditised processes but not for all.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">The "modular" ERP System</mark>

For example, a <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">service company probably has no need for a manufacturing</mark> module.  

Other companies <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">may already have a system</mark> that they believe to be adequate. Generally speaking, the <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">greater the number of modules selected, the greater the integration benefits</mark>, but also the greater the costs, risks and changes involved.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP Rollout Approaches – Big Bang</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">The entire ERP system goes live in all departments, locations, and processes on a single date.:</mark>  
- All legacy systems are switched off at once.  
- All modules (Finance, HR, Supply Chain, Manufacturing, etc.) go live simultaneously.  
- Requires very intense preparation and testing.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Advantages:</mark> 
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Fast transformation:</mark> shorter overall project timeline.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Single transition:</mark> avoids running old and new systems in parallel.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Immediate integration:</mark> real-time data available across the organisation from day one.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Risks:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">High risk of failure:</mark> any major issue impacts the whole organisation.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Steep learning curve:</mark> staff must adapt quickly.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Limited buffer:</mark> no gradual feedback cycles.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Suitability:</mark>
- Medium-sized organisations.  
- When processes are standardised across all departments.  
- When the cost of parallel systems is too high.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP Rollout Approaches – Phased</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Modules, business units, or geographic locations go live step by step, not all at once:</mark>  
- By Function / Module e.g., Finance → Procurement → HR → Manufacturing.  
- By Geography e.g., Ireland → UK → Europe → Global.  
- By Business Unit e.g., Group of stores, divisions, product lines.  
- By Process e.g., Order-to-Cash first, then Procure-to-Pay.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Advantages:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Lower risk:</mark> impact isolated to one area at a time.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Simplified change management:</mark> staff can be trained gradually.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Early feedback:</mark> issues discovered in early phases can be fixed before later rollouts.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Risks:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Longer timeline:</mark> full deployment takes more time.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Temporary integrations:</mark> may need interfaces between legacy and new systems.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Inconsistency:</mark> different teams may temporarily use different processes.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Suitability:</mark>
- Large or global organisations.  
- Organisations with diverse business units.  
- Situations with high risk if operations fail (e.g., hospitals, logistics).

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP Rollout Approaches – Parallel</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Both the old system and the new ERP run together for a defined period:</mark>  
- Users enter or verify transactions in both systems.  
- Organisation evaluates accuracy and performance of the new ERP before fully switching.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Advantages:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Very low risk:</mark> if ERP errors occur, the legacy system acts as a backup.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Helps user training:</mark> staff can compare systems side-by-side.  
- Gradual confidence-building.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Risks:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Double work:</mark> users must duplicate entries.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Costly:</mark> requires supporting two systems at once.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Delays:</mark> mistakes or mismatches can slow progress.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Suitability:</mark>
- Finance modules requiring high accuracy (e.g., payroll, general ledger).  
- Highly regulated industries.  
- Organisations with strict audit or compliance requirements.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP Rollout Approaches – Pilot</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Rollout begins with a small group, department, or location to test the ERP before organisation-wide deployment:</mark> 
- One plant or team goes live first.  
- They become the "pilot site."  
- Lessons learned are applied to future rollouts.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Advantages:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Controlled testing:</mark> real-world environment without full exposure.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Feedback loop:</mark> configuration and training improved before expansion.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">User champions:</mark> pilot users can train others.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Risks:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Slow scale-up:</mark> initial pilot phase can take longer.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Non-representative pilot:</mark> if pilot group is unusual, future issues may still arise.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Pilot fatigue:</mark> pilot team may become overburdened.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Suitability:</mark>
- Organisations with varied operations.  
- Projects introducing major process redesign.  
- When executive teams want early proof of value

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Deployment Model and Ownership</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>
- Cloud ERP systems are hosted and maintained by the vendor in their own data centres. 
- Organisations access the system over the internet using a subscription model (usually monthly or annual).  

The <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">vendor</mark> manages the hardware, storage, networking, security, updates, and system performance.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark> On-premise ERP systems are installed on the organisation’s own servers and operated within their own IT environment.  

The <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">organisation</mark> owns and maintains everything: hardware, software, upgrades, and security.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cost Structure</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- Operates on an <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">operational expenditure model (OPEX)</mark>.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">No upfront investment</mark> in servers or infrastructure.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Subscription</mark> includes maintenance, updates, and support.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Lower initial cost</mark> but continuous recurring fees.  

Best suited for organisations wanting predictable long-term costs and minimal IT overhead.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- Operates on a <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">capital expenditure model (CAPEX)</mark>.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Significant upfront investment</mark> in hardware and licenses.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Ongoing costs</mark> include IT staff, maintenance, security, and upgrades.  

Useful where long-term ownership and control outweigh initial cost concerns.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customisation & Flexibility</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- Typically offers <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">standardised functionality</mark> with configuration rather than heavy customisation.  
- Updates may limit highly bespoke changes, as the <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">vendor controls the codebase</mark>.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Faster deployment</mark> because of pre-built workflows.  

Strong choice for organisations willing to adopt industry best-practice processes.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- Allows for <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">extensive customisation</mark>, including modifications to source code.  
- Organisations can <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">tailor workflows</mark> down to the smallest detail.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">More complex</mark> implementations and longer timelines.  

Better for unique or highly specialised business processes.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Security and Data Control</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Vendors invest heavily</mark> in cyber-security, often exceeding what individual organisations can achieve alone.  
- Data is stored externally, which can raise <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">compliance or sovereignty concerns</mark>.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Security responsibilities</mark> follow the shared responsibility model: the vendor protects the infrastructure, and the client controls user access and data practices.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Full control</mark> over data storage, access, and security policies.  
- Favoured in <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">industries with strict compliance</mark> (defence, healthcare, government).  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Greater responsibility:</mark> the organisation must handle backups, patching, and monitoring.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Scalability and Performance</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Highly scalable:</mark> organisations can add users, storage, or features instantly.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Ideal for growing businesses</mark>, multi-site organisations, or global expansions.  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Performance is dependent</mark> on internet connectivity and vendor infrastructure.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Scaling requires new servers</mark>, hardware upgrades, and potential downtime.  
- Offers <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">predictable performance</mark> in environments with stable, internal networks.  
- Best suited for <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">organisations with steady workloads</mark> and predictable size.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Upgrades and Innovation</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- Automatic, frequent updates delivered by the vendor.  
- New features, AI enhancements, and security patches arrive continuously.  
- Reduces risk of systems becoming outdated.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- Upgrades must be planned, budgeted for, and executed manually.  
- Organisations may delay upgrades due to cost, leading to "ERP version stagnation".  
- Highly customised systems face upgrade complications

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Implementation Speed</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- Much faster to implement because infrastructure is already in place.  
- Preconfigured industry templates speed up deployment.  
- Often paired with hybrid methodologies.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- Longer timelines due to hardware setup, customisation, and testing.  
- Typically suited to structured methodologies (e.g. Waterfall)

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Suitability</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cloud ERP:</mark>  
- Small-to-medium organisations  
- Rapidly growing companies  
- Businesses adopting standardised processes  
- Companies without large IT departments  
- Distributed teams needing remote access  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On Premise ERP:</mark>
- Highly regulated industries  
- Organisations needing extreme customisation  
- Businesses requiring tight control over data  
- Legacy-heavy environments with complex integrations

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">ERP Implementation Risk Factors</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Implementation Risk Factors:</mark>
- No clear goal
- Lack of clear planning
- Under-estimating resources required
- Customisations 
- Insufficient testing

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">No Clear Goal</mark>

Often, <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">lack of consensus</mark> on the problems being solved, the outcome desired, or the specific financial justification of the project, leads to challenges later controlling the scope and maintaining executive sponsorship.  

Having a clear destination means defining the important business processes and financial benefits.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Lack of Detailed Planning</mark>  

All projects of this size start with some kind of plan. However, more times than not, the plan is not realistic, detailed, or specific enough.  

To be a good plan, it needs to identify all the requirements and the people who are going to work on them.  

It needs to be at a level of detail where a knowledgeable person can visualise the work, usually in work blocks of a few days.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Under-Estimating Resources Required</mark>

Having a solid understanding of the internal and external resources needed to complete the project is critical. E.g.:  

For internal resources, understanding the time commitment needed from business users, typically in the Finance, Accounting, or Human Resources departments, is one of the most commonly underestimated areas.  

For external resources, having an agreement up-front with your consultants and contractors about the specific duration, skills, and quantity of resources needed is critical.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customisations</mark>

Customisations, along with interfaces and data conversion, are the main areas of technical risk in ERP implementations.  

Customisations always start out small, but incrementally grow to become the technical challenges that derail these projects.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Insufficient testing</mark>

When schedules get tight, reducing the number and depth of test cycles is often one of the first areas that often gets cut.  

The purpose of testing in an ERP project is not to see if the software works. The purpose is to see if the system meets your business needs and produces the output you need.  

Reducing testing may not leave defects undiscovered, but it certainly increases the risk the ERP system will be missing important functions or not be well accepted by end users.

# <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">09 Customer Relationship Management</mark>

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customer Relationship Management (CRM)</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">An enterprise wide effort to acquire and retain customers:</mark> 
- Includes a <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">one-to-one relationship</mark> between a customer  and a seller.
- One simple idea <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">"Treat different customers differently"</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Helps keep profitable customers and maximises lifetime revenue from them:</mark>
- Identify/acquire/retain most profitable prospects  
- Acquisition is far more expensive that retention.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Integrating information</mark> from sales, marketing, customer service and any other service points

Also known as <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">touch points</mark> – where the customer interacts with the organisation  

Therefore must provide analytical tools for <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">determining value, loyalty, profitability of customers</mark>  

Assist in <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">acquiring new customers</mark>, providing better service and support to customers, customise offerings to customer preferences, provide ongoing value to retain profitable customers

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">The CRM Cycle</mark> 

CRM is a continuous process of optimising interactions with the customer and seeking new opportunities to increase sales

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">1. Identify:</mark> Identify the customer

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">2. Differentiate:</mark> Analyse and differentiate customer behaviour to identify propensity to buy specific products.  
  
<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">3. Customise:</mark> Customise customer plan to optimise revenue  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">4. Interact:</mark> Customise customer "touch points" (interaction) to optimise revenue

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customer Relationship Management (CRM) - Technical requirements</mark>  
  
A <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">cross-functional enterprise system</mark> that integrates and <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">automates many of the processes</mark> in sales, marketing & customer service that interact with customers

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Loose Coupling</mark>  

When two systems are tightly coupled there are a set of requirements that are forced into play

![[Pasted image 20260324175316.png]]

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Requirements:</mark>
- Data encoding used by both systems has to be the same
- The location of the system to communicate with is known  
- The system that needs to be communicated with is currently up and running  
- The two systems communicate using a common protocol/format  

To change from a tightly coupled situation to a loosely coupled situation we need to remove the these requirements.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Location:</mark> introduce a level of indirection so that systems can be located / moved without the need for the other system to be aware.

![[Pasted image 20260324175439.png]]
 
<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Time:</mark> introduce a temporary storage mechanism so that systems can send / receive content asynchronously. Receiving system does not need to up and running at the time of the request but it will still be processed without error.

![[Pasted image 20260324175554.png]]

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Format:</mark> introduce a mechanism to allow systems use their own data formats. Each system uses their own format but content is transformed / validated as needed without the systems themselves being aware.

![[Pasted image 20260324175645.png]]

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Loose Coupling:</mark>
- Reduces brittleness
- Allows scalability 
- Can allow additional processing without the need for systems development

An Architecture  

Integrate various applications over a bus-like infrastructure

Decouple applications from each other by providing middleware to handle communication paths and workflows

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Integration Principles</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Orchestration:</mark> Composing several existing fine-grained components into a single higher order composite service. This can be done to achieve appropriate "granularity" of services and promote reuse and manageability of the underlying components.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Transformation:</mark> Data transformation between canonical data formats and specific data formats required by each ESB connector. An example of this would be transforming between CSV, Cobol copybook or EDI formats to either SOAP/XML or JSON. Canonical data formats can greatly simplify the transformation requirements associated with a large ESB implementation where there are many consumers and providers, each with their own data formats and definitions.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Transportation:</mark> Transport protocol negotiation between multiple formats (such as HTTP, JMS, JDBC). <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Note:</mark> Mule treats databases like another "service" by making JDBC just another transport (or endpoint) where data can be accessed.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Mediation:</mark> Providing multiple interfaces for the purpose of a) supporting multiple versions of a service for backwards compatibility or alternatively, b) to allow for multiple channels to the same underlying component implementation. This second requirement may involve providing multiple interfaces to the same component, one legacy interface (flat file) and one standards compliant (SOAP/XML) interface.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Non-functional consistency:</mark> For a typical ESB initiative, this can include consistency around the way security and monitoring policies are applied and implemented. Additionally the goals of scalability and availability can be achieved by using multiple instances of an ESB to provide increased throughput (scalability) and eliminate single-points-of-failure (SPOFs), which is the key objective for highly available systems.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">I. Information Systems (IS) Foundations</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Definition of an Information System (IS):</mark> An organised combination of people, hardware, software, communication networks, data resources, and policies and procedures that stores, retrieves, transforms, and disseminates information in an organisation.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Fundamental Roles of IS in Business:</mark>
- Support Business Processes and Operations.
- Support Business Decision Making.
- Support Strategies for Competitive Advantage.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Categories of Information Systems:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Operations Support Systems:</mark> Efficiently process business transactions, control industrial processes, support communication and collaboration, and update corporate databases.
	- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Transaction Processing Systems:</mark> Record and process business transactions (e.g., sales processing).
	- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Process Control Systems:</mark> Monitor and control physical processes (e.g., refinery sensors).
	- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Enterprise Collaboration Systems:</mark> Enhance team and workgroup communication (e.g., email, video meetings).
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Management Support Systems:</mark> Provide information as reports and displays and give direct computer support to managers during decision-making.
	- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Management Information Systems (MIS):</mark> Prespecified reporting for managers (e.g., daily sales analysis).
	- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Decision Support Systems (DSS):</mark> Interactive and ad hoc support (e.g., what-if analysis).
	- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Executive Information Systems (EIS):</mark> Critical information tailored for executives (e.g., actions of competitors).

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">IS Components:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Resources:</mark> People (specialists and end users), Hardware (machines and media), Software (programs and procedures), Data (product descriptions, customer records), and Network (communications media and processors).
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Activities:</mark> Input (data entry), Processing (calculations, sorting), Output (reports, forms), Storage (databases), and Control (monitoring performance).

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Measuring IT Success:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Efficiency:</mark> Minimise cost, time, and use of information resources; "doing things right."
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Effectiveness:</mark> Support business strategies, enable processes, and increase customer value; "doing the right things."

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">II. Business Strategy & Competitive Advantage</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Porter’s Five Forces Model: To succeed, a business must develop strategies to counter:</mark> 
1. Rivalry of competitors,
2. Threat of new entrants, 
3. Threat of substitutes, 
4. Bargaining power of customers,
5. Bargaining power of suppliers.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Five Competitive Strategies:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cost Leadership:</mark> Become low-cost producers or help suppliers/customers reduce costs.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Differentiation:</mark> Differentiate products from competitors or focus on a particular segment or niche.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Innovation:</mark> Create unique products/services or make radical changes to business processes
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Growth:</mark> Expand capacity, global markets, or diversify into new products
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Alliance:</mark> Establish linkages and alliances with customers, suppliers, and competitors (e.g., virtual companies).

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">The Value Chain:</mark> Views the company as a chain of basic activities that add value to its products and services.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Primary Processes:</mark> Directly relate to manufacturing or delivering products (e.g., Inbound Logistics, Operations, Outbound Logistics, Marketing, Customer Service).
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Support Processes:</mark> Help support the day-to-day running of the company (e.g., Administrative Coordination, HR Management, Technology Development, Procurement).

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">III. Enterprise Business Systems (ERP, SCM, CRM)</mark>

#### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">1. Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Definition:</mark> A cross-functional enterprise backbone that integrates and automates processes within Manufacturing, Logistics, Finance, and HR. It focuses on value chains rather than individual functions.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Core Capabilities:</mark> Integrated modules, common process/data models, and a common database where updating one module automatically updates others.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Key Modules:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Finance:</mark> Core of many systems; gathers data from all functional departments and generates quarterly financial statements.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Purchasing:</mark> Streamlines procurement of raw materials; tightly integrated with inventory control and production planning.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Sales and Marketing:</mark> Supports order placement, scheduling, shipping, and invoicing.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Rollout Approaches:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Big Bang:</mark> Entire system goes live in all departments on a single date; legacy systems switched off at once.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Phased:</mark> Modules or locations go live step by step (e.g., by Function, Geography, or Process).
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Parallel:</mark> Old and new systems run together for a period to evaluate accuracy.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Pilot:</mark> Rollout begins with a small group/site to test before wide deployment.

2. Differentiate behaviour/propensity to buy,
3. Customise the customer plan, and

- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customer Service:</mark> Managing requests for service (e.g., Case Management).

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">IV. Enterprise Application Integration (EAI)</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Loose Coupling:</mark> The goal is to provide Location, Time, and Format independence.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Location:</mark> Indirection so systems can be moved without others being aware.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Time:</mark> Asynchronous messaging so receiving systems don't need to be up at the time of the request.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Format:</mark> Mechanism to allow systems to use their own data formats via transformation.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Message-Oriented Middleware (MOM):</mark> Software acting as a reliable middleman.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Point-to-Point:</mark> Based on message queues; each message has only one consumer.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Publish-Subscribe:</mark> Based on topics; each subscriber receives a copy of the message.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Enterprise Service Bus (ESB):</mark> An architecture that integrates various applications over a bus-like infrastructure.
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Integration Principles:</mark> Orchestration (composing services), Transformation (data conversion), Transportation (protocol negotiation), and Mediation (multiple interfaces).
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Common Patterns:</mark>
	- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Content-Based Router:</mark> Passes messages based on internal information.
	- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Splitter:</mark> Breaks a composite message into individual messages.
	- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Message Translator:</mark> Translates formats while in transit.
	- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Aggregator:</mark> Combines separate but related messages.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">V. AI in the Enterprise</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Predictive AI:</mark> Analysis of large datasets to forecast potential scenarios (e.g., demand forecasting in ERP, inventory management in SCM).

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Generative AI (GenAI):</mark> Interprets and generates language content (e.g., Copilot in CRM for summarising conversations or auto-generating responses).

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):</mark> Enables LLMs to retrieve relevant information from external databases in real-time, overcoming outdated training data.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">VI. Integration Technologies (XML, XSD, XSL)</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">XML:</mark> A language designed to carry data where tags are not predefined.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">XSD (XML Schema):</mark> An XML document that defines the content and structure (vocabulary and rules) of XML documents.
- Simple Types: Contain only character data (text).
- Complex Types: Contain attributes or child elements.
- Compositors: Define how element lists are treated: sequence (fixed order), choice (any one), or all (any order).

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">XSLT:</mark> A stylesheet language used to transform XML content into another format (e.g., HTML or another XML schema).

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">XPath:</mark> A declarative language used to locate nodes within an XML node tree (e.g., root, element, attribute, and text nodes)
