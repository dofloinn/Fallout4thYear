“the ability to learn and perform suitable techniques to solve problems and achieve goals, appropriate to the context in an uncertain, ever-varying world”  

“an umbrella term for a range of algorithm-based technologies that solve complex tasks by carrying out functions that previously required human thinking”  

“technology that enables computers and machines to simulate human intelligence and problem-solving capabilities”

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Deep Learning</mark>

Deep learning is a branch of machine learning that uses Artificial Neural Networks (algorithms loosely modelled on the way the human brain works) to learn from large amounts of data.  

Training with large amounts of data is what configures the neurons in the neural network.  

Deep learning models can recognise complex patterns in pictures, text, sounds, and other data to produce accurate insights and predictions.  

Once trained, these models can be used to make decisions about patterns they have not seen before.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">AI - Weak v Strong</mark> 

Weak AI (or Narrow AI) is designed to perform specific tasks, such as facial recognition, object detection, recommender systems  

Strong AI aims to develop machines that possess human-like intelligence and have the ability to think and reason.  

The ultimate goal is to achieve this human like “General Artificial Intelligence"

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Data</mark> 

AI algorithms typically learn by examining existing data and making a model  

The more data the better the model  

Hence all the interest in “Big Data”  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Data can come from anywhere (the algorithms don’t care):</mark>  
- Blood tests → cancer outcomes
- Images → object identification 
- Insurance history → risk assessment

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Retrieval-Augmented Generation - RAG:</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">The Issue:</mark> LLMs are trained on vast amounts of data but this data has limitations. It can become outdated and the models might lack specific knowledge needed for certain tasks. 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">RAG:</mark> RAG addresses this by enabling LLMs to retrieve relevant information from external databases, documents, or other knowledge sources in real-time.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">RAG in the Enterprise</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Internal information/knowledge EXAMPLE:</mark>
- Large company with many offices around the world - internal documents, policies, and research artefacts.  
- Employees waste a lot of time trying to find the information they need and sometimes do work that's already been done. 
- Use RAG to create a smart internal search engine.
- This search engine indexes all the company's documents, so they can be searched easily. 
- Employees can ask questions using normal, everyday language, and RAG will find and summarize the most relevant information. 
- RAG also provides links to the original documents, so employees can double-check the information. 
- This leads to employees being more productive, faster access to important information, less duplicated work, better teamwork and knowledge sharing, and easier compliance with company policies.

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Chatbots - Example:</mark> 
- Large online store that gets large amounts of customer questions every day.
- Regular chatbots often don't understand complicated or specific questions, which makes customers frustrated and creates a lot of extra work for the support team. 
- Use RAG to connect the chatbot to all the company's product information, frequently asked questions, and records of past customer interactions. 
- When a customer asks a question, RAG finds the most relevant information from those sources.
- Chatbot uses that information to give accurate and helpful answers. 
- Results in fewer support tickets, faster and better customer responses, happier customers, and everyone getting consistent and up-to-date information.
