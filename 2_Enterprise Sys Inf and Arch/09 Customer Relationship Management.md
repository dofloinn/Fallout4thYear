### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customer Relationship Management (CRM)</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">An enterprise wide effort to acquire and retain customers:</mark> 
- Includes a <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">one-to-one relationship</mark> between a customer  and a seller.
- One simple idea <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">“Treat different customers differently”</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Helps keep profitable customers and maximizes lifetime revenue from them:</mark>
- Identify/acquire/retain most profitable prospects  
- Acquisition is far more expensive that retention.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Integrating information</mark> from sales, marketing, customer service and any other service points  

Also known as <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">touch points</mark> – where the customer interacts with the organisation  

Therefore must provide analytical tools for <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">determining value, loyalty, profitability of customers</mark>  

Assist in <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">acquiring new customers</mark>, providing better service and support to customers, customize offerings to customer preferences, provide ongoing value to retain profitable customers

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">The CRM Cycle</mark> 

CRM is a continuous process of optimising interactions with the customer and seeking new opportunities to increase sales

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">1. Identify:</mark> Identify the customer

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">2. Differentiate:</mark> Analyse and differentiate customer behaviour to identify propensity to buy specific products.  
  
<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">3. Customise:</mark> Customise customer plan to optimise revenue  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">4. Interact:</mark> Customise customer “touch points” (interaction) to optimise revenue

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customer Relationship Management (CRM) - Technical requirements</mark>  
  
A <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">cross-functional enterprise system</mark> that integrates and <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">automates many of the processes</mark> in sales, marketing & customer service that interact with customers  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customer touchpoints:</mark>
- These processes may be in functional/departmental systems; e.g. call centres, web-sites  
- Therefore, CRM is <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">similar to ERP in impact</mark> on organisation – and face similar issues related to organisational readiness  

Provides <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">analytic capabilities</mark> to optimise the customer relationship across all touchpoints 

Ideally <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">should be tied in with ERP</mark> or other enterprise systems within an organisation

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">CRM Challenges</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Data model integration and synchronisation:</mark>  
- CRM gathers customer touchpoint data into a single system for analysis  
- CRM should ensure consistency of the data across the systems  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">On-going data synchronisation and propagation:</mark>  
- CRM must ensure that data <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">remains synchronised</mark> through <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">on-going customer change</mark> (e.g. Change of address); is implemented via a data mart
- Outputs from <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">analysis/planning</mark> must be <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">propagated</mark> back into individual functional/departmental systems (e.g. marketing, sales)  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Note:</mark> If a ‘global’ ERP has already been implemented, CRM can be more easily implemented over (with) the ERP  

Data <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">integration</mark> stage and data synchronisation should <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">already be in place</mark> in the ERP

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Examples of CRM Functional Modules</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Account Management:</mark>
- Helps sales, marketing & service professionals 
- Capture & track data about past/planned contacts with customers/prospects  
- E.g. Dynamics Unified Profile and Case Management

![[Pasted image 20260321163758.png]]

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Sales Force Automation (SFA)</mark>
- Provides sales reps with software tools & data they need to <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">support & manage sales activities</mark> 
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Cross-selling</mark> is trying to sell a customer of one product with a related product  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Up-selling</mark> is trying to sell customer a better product than they are currently seeking 
- E.g. Dynamics “Customer Journeys”

![[Pasted image 20260321163859.png]]

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Direct Marketing:</mark>
- Help marketing professionals accomplish direct marketing campaigns by tasks such as qualifying leads for targeted marketing & scheduling & tracking direct marketing mailings 
- E.g. Dynamics Segments and Customer Service

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Retention and Loyalty Programs:</mark>  
- Try to help a company identify, reward, & market to their most loyal and profitable customers
- Data mining tools & analytical software
- Centralised Customer Database 
- E.g. Dynamics Unified Profile

![[Pasted image 20260321164109.png]]

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Customer Service and Support:</mark>
- Provides sales reps with software tools & <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">database access to customer database shared</mark> by sales & marketing professions

- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Call centre software</mark> routes calls to customer support agents based upon their skills and type of call  
- <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Help desk software</mark> provides relevant service data & suggestions for resolving problems for customer service reps helping customers with problems

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">The Business Benefits, Value and Potential Disadvantages</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">CRM Benefits:</mark>
- Single view of customer data
- Better knowledge of customers 
- Better understanding of customer needs 
- Knowledge retention improved

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Business Value of CRM:</mark>
- Increased customer satisfaction
- Reduced direct marketing costs
- More effective marketing
- Lower costs for customer acquisition and retention  
- Increased sales revenue by identifying profitable customers and segments for focused marketing and cross-selling  
- Reduced churn rate (number of customers who stop using or purchasing products or services)

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Drawbacks / Risks of implementing a CRM</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Difficult and expensive implementation (similar to those of ERP) due to:</mark>
- Integration challenges  
- Business change required  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Scalability</mark> an issue due to the potential scale of data available  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">No guarantee on effectiveness:</mark> Can be hard to prove that CRM works because CRM cannot be separated from everyday operations to assess impact

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Other Advantages</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Discover New Customers:</mark>
- CRM systems help the organisation in identifying potential customers. By keeping a track of the profiles of their existing clientele, the business can easily come up with a strategy to determine the kind of people they should target such that it returns them maximum opportunity.
- For example, if several students in a university sign up for a particular mobile service provider, the business can come up with a strategy to target rest of the community in the university.  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Simplify Marketing And Sales Processes:</mark>  
- CRM helps in developing better communication channels. Interactive Voice Response System, web sites, etc. have made life easy both for the organisation and also for its sales representatives. 
- It allows the business to give its customers the option of choosing how they want to communicate with the business.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Case Study</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Lenovo:</mark>
- Global powerhouse in technology solutions  
- 77,000 employees  
- Serves 180 markets around the world (30+ manufacturing facilities)  
- 2020 - disparate systems (which included Salesforce and SAP crm systems) that lacked a centralized and harmonized data source  
- 2022 - two-year rollout of single Dynamics 365 CRM system -> “..historic sales”  
- Integrated with SAP ERP  
- $1.3 billion increase in potential annual global sales with streamlined sales processes enabled by Dynamics 365  
- $4 million per year in licensing costs saved by consolidating or decommissioning 52 third-party apps
