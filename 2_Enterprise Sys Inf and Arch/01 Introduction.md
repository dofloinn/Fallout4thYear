### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Business / Enterprise</mark>  

What is an Enterprise?

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Definition:</mark>  
1. An undertaking, especially one of some scope, complication, and risk.  
2. A business organisation.  
3. Industrious, systematic activity, especially when directed toward profit: Private enterprise is basic to capitalism.  
4. Willingness to undertake new ventures; initiative  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Example:</mark>
- Walmart 2025 – total revenue of $681 billion
- Employs more than 2.1 million associates around the world.
- Early adopters of Information Technology

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Our definition</mark>

An enterprise is an organization that uses computers...  

That could mean big, small or complex organisations: small businesses, SME, multi-national corporations.  

Note: Enterprise Systems in the computing industry generally refers to large corporations.

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Types of Operations Support Systems</mark> 

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Transaction Processing Systems:</mark>
- Record and process business transactions
- Examples: sales processing, inventory systems, accounting systems  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Process Control Systems:</mark>
- Monitor and control physical processes
- Example: using sensors to monitor chemical processes in a petroleum refinery  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Enterprise Collaboration Systems:</mark>
- Enhance team and workgroup communication
- Examples: email, video meetings, chat software, integrated social media

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Types of Management Support Systems</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Management Information Systems (MIS):</mark>
- Reports and displays
- Example: daily sales analysis reports  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Decision Support Systems (DSS):</mark>
- Interactive and ad hoc support
- Example: a what-if analysis to determine where to spend advertising funds  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Executive Information Systems (EIS):</mark>
- Critical information for executives and managers
- Example: easy access to actions of competitors

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Other Information Systems</mark>  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Expert Systems:</mark>
- Provide expert advice
- Example: credit application advisor, bespoke systems, AI trained systems  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Knowledge Management Systems:</mark>
- Support creation, organization, and dissemination of business knowledge throughout company
- Example: intranet access to best business practices, ERP & CRM modules  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Strategic Information Systems:</mark>
- Help get a strategic advantage over competitor
- Example: online shipment tracking, SCM modules  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Functional Business Systems:</mark>
- Focus on operational and managerial applications of basic business functions
- Examples: accounting, finance, or marketing - ERP

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">What is a System?</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">A system is:</mark>
- A set of interrelated components
- With a clearly defined boundary
- Working together to achieve a common set of objectives  

By accepting inputs and producing outputs in an organised transformation process

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Basic Functions of a System</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Input:</mark> Capturing and assembling elements that  
enter the system to be processed  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Processing:</mark> Transformation process that converts input into output  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Output:</mark> Transferring transformed elements to their ultimate destination

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Data Versus Information</mark>

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Data</mark> are raw facts about physical phenomena or business transactions  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Information</mark> is data that has been converted into meaningful and useful context for end users  

<mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Examples:</mark>
- Sales data is names, quantities, and euro amounts
- Sales information is amount of sales by product type, sales territory, or salesperson

### <mark style="background: #00ECFFA6;">Recognising Information Systems</mark>

Business professionals should be able to look at an information system and identify...  

The people, hardware, software, data, and network resources they use  

The type of information products they produce  

The way they perform input, processing, output, storage, and control activities
