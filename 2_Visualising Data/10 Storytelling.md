### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">The Big Idea</mark>

The <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Big Idea:</mark> the single most important insight  

It should be expressible in one sentence  

“Countries with higher GDP tend to live longer, but the relationship plateaus at high income levels.”

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Why it matters</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Guides:</mark>  
- Chart selection  
- Encoding decisions  
- Narrative structure  

Prevents Chart Dumping

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Big Idea</mark>

A big idea is a central theme that drives decision-making

It presents a compelling narrative, making complex data accessible

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> Sustainable cities need better public transportation data

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">From Exploratory -> Explanatory</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Pipeline:</mark>  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Exploration:</mark> Find patterns, Try multiple encodings
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Selection:</mark> Choose ONE key message
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Explanation:</mark> Build visuals to support it

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Key point:</mark> Not everything you discover belongs in the final visualisation. 

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Narrative Approaches Linear Narrative (Slide / Report Style)</mark>  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Characteristics:</mark> 
- Step-by-step progression  
- Each chart builds on the previous  
- Controlled order  
- Use when  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Teaching / explaining clearly:</mark>
- Presentations  
- Reports  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Pros:</mark>
- High clarity  
- Low cognitive load  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Cons:</mark>
- Low interactivity  
- Passive user

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Narrative Approaches - Scrollytelling (Narrative + Interaction)</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Characteristics:</mark>
- Story unfolds as user scrolls  
- Animations / transitions  
- Focused attention  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Use when:</mark>
- You want engagement + explanation  
- Complex stories with progression  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Pros:</mark>
- High engagement  
- Good for complex narratives  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Cons:</mark>
- Can overload users  
- Harder to design well

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Narrative Approaches - Dashboard (Exploration First)</mark>  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Characteristics:</mark>
- Multiple views at once  
- User-driven exploration  
- Filters / controls  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Use when:</mark>
- Monitoring / decision-making  
- Multiple questions, not one story  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Pros:</mark>
- Flexible  
- Powerful for analysis  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Cons:</mark>
- No single narrative  
- Risk of overload

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">A dashboard</mark>

A way of displaying various types of visual data in one place.  

Should convey different, but related information in an easy-to-digest form.  

Combines charts, tables and statements of fact, to complete a story.  

May include key performance indicators (KPIs).

![[Pasted image 20260330162830.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Operational Dashboard</mark> 

Focuses on day-to-day operations and performance monitoring.  

Used by department managers and frontline employees.  

Helps in real-time decision-making and process improvements.  

Data is frequently updated (real-time, daily, weekly).

![[Pasted image 20260330162917.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Analytic Dashboard</mark>  

Focuses on exploring data trends, patterns, and insights.  

Used by data analysts, business intelligence teams, and decision-makers.  

Helps in identifying root causes, forecasting trends, and making data-driven decisions.  

Data includes historical analysis, correlations, and sometimes predictive models.

![[Pasted image 20260330162949.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Geographic online dashboards</mark>

Can be interactive  

Can be animated over time.  

Used for sales, geography, demographics.

![[Pasted image 20260330163012.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Strategic Dashboard</mark>

Focuses on long-term goals and high-level performance.  

Used by executives and senior managers.  

Helps track business strategy, trends, and overall success.  

Data is aggregated and updated periodically (monthly, quarterly, yearly).

![[Pasted image 20260330163049.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">KPI Dashboard</mark>

Can have multiple pages and be interactive.  

Can also be dynamic, updating at fixed time intervals.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Imagine a retail company:</mark>
- A strategic dashboard would show annual revenue trends, customer retention, and profitability.  
- An operational dashboard would track daily store sales, inventory levels, and staffing efficiency.  
- An analytic dashboard would analyse why customer churn is increasing and predict future sales trends.

![[Pasted image 20260330163135.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Catalog Online dashboards</mark>

Organizes, displays, and allows users to explore a collection of data assets, products, content, or services in a structured and visually engaging manner.  

Often provides filtering, sorting, and search capabilities to help users quickly find relevant information.

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Perceptual Accuracy</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">RANKING OF VISUAL ACCURACY (CLEVELAND AND MCGILL):</mark>
- Position (best)
- Length  
- Angle  
- Area
- Colour (least precise)  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">IMPLICATIONS:</mark> Use position/length for key comparisons 

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Avoid:</mark>  
- Pie charts for precise comparison  
- Area for subtle differences

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Colour Theory in Visualisation - Hue</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Hue (What colour is it ?)</mark>  
- Type of colour  
- Position on the colour wheel  

Use hue for categorical data  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example: Continents → different hues</mark>  
- Europe = blue  
- Asia = red  
- Africa = green  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Common mistake:</mark> Using different hues to represent ordered data - e.g. low = blue, medium = green, high = red. This implies categories, not a continuous scale  

Hue separates categories — not magnitude.

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Colour Theory in Visualisation - Saturation</mark> 

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Saturation (How intense is the colour?)</mark>  
- Saturation = colour intensity / purity  
- Intensity / emphasis  
- From greyed-out → vivid  
- Highlight vs background  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Emphasis:</mark>
- Highlight important data:  
- High saturation → focus  
- Low saturation → background  
- Layering information  
- Foreground vs background distinction  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Common mistake:</mark> Everything is high saturation → nothing stands out  

Saturation controls attention.

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Colour Theory in Visualisation - Value</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">C. Value (Brightness/Lightness):</mark>
- Value = how light or dark a colour is  
- Ordered data (low → high)  
- Use value for ordered / numeric data  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark>
- Population density:  
- Light = low  
- Dark = high  
- Dark blue → light blue  
- Black → grey → white  

Value communicates magnitude.

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">2. Limit Hues</mark>  
- Too many hues → confusion
- Aim for ≤ 5–7 categories

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">3. Use Saturation Intentionally</mark>  
- Highlight key data only  
- Everything else → muted
 
 <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">4. Be careful with value contrast</mark>  
- Ensure readability, Especially for: 
- Text  
- Overlaid elements

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Cognitive Load: The mental effort required to interpret a visual</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">SOURCES OF OVERLOAD:</mark> 
- Too many charts
- Too many encodings
- Poor layout
- Unclear hierarchy  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">TECHNIQUES TO REDUCE LOAD:</mark>  
- Progressive disclosure  
- Highlighting  
- Annotation  
- Consistent scales

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Annotation and Guidance</mark> 

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Essential for explanatory visuals</mark>
- Labels  
- Arrows  
- Highlight regions  

“Don’t make the user work to find the insight.”

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Every design decision should answer:</mark>  
- Does this support the Big Idea?
- Does this reduce or increase cognitive load?
- Is this perceptually accurate?

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">ACCENT Principles</mark>

The essence of a graph is the clear communication of quantitative information.  

The ACCENT principles emphasize, or accent, six aspects which determine the effectiveness of a visual display for portraying data.  

The ACCENT principles guide the creation of clear, effective, and ethical visualizations.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Always ask:</mark> Does this visualisation communicate the message clearly, efficiently, and truthfully?

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Apprehension:</mark>
- Ability to correctly perceive relations among variables.  
- Does the graph maximize apprehension of the relations among variables?  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Clarity:</mark>
- Ability to visually distinguish all the elements of a graph.
- Are the most important elements or relations visually most prominent?

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Consistency:</mark>
- Ability to interpret a graph based on similarity to previous graphs.  
- Are the elements, symbol shapes and colours consistent with their use in previous graphs?

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Efficiency:</mark>
- Ability to portray a possibly complex relation in as simple a way as possible.  
- Are the elements of the graph economically used?  
- Is the graph easy to interpret?  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Necessity:</mark>
- The need for the graph, and the graphical elements.  
- Is the graph a more useful way to represent the data than alternatives (table, text)?  
- Are all the graph elements necessary to convey the relations?  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Truthfulness:</mark>
- Ability to determine the true value represented by any graphical element by its magnitude relative to the implicit or explicit scale.  
- Are the graph elements accurately positioned and scaled?
