### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Explanatory v Exploratory Visualization</mark>  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Explanatory Data Visualization:</mark> 
- Designed to communicate specific insights clearly.  
- Often used in reports, presentations, and dashboards.  
- Focuses on clarity, simplicity, and storytelling.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Examples:</mark>  
- Infographics summarizing key takeaways.  
- Dashboards showing business performance.  
- Line charts illustrating trends over time in reports.

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Exploratory Data Visualization:</mark> 
- Used for analysing and discovering patterns in data.  
- Often interactive and flexible for deeper exploration.  
- Used by analysts and data scientists during the analysis process.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Examples:</mark>  
- Scatter plots to explore relationships.  
- Histograms to examine distributions.  
- Box plots to detect outliers

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Types of Visualizations by Variable Count</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Univariate Visualizations:</mark>
- Display a single variable.
- Examples: Histograms, box plots.
- Sample use case: Understanding the distribution of salaries in a company.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Bivariate Visualizations:</mark>  
- Compare two variables to identify relationships.
- Examples: Scatterplots, line charts.
- Sample use case: Exploring the relationship between temperature and ice cream sales.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Multivariate Visualizations:</mark>  
- Show relationships between three or more variables.
- Examples: Heatmaps, bubble charts, parallel coordinate plots.
- Sample use case: Analysing customer demographics, spending habits, and purchase frequency together.

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Iconic Memory</mark>

A brief visual memory store (~250-500ms).  

Captures large amounts of raw visual data before conscious attention.  

Helps create smooth visual perception and object recognition.  

Good design works with iconic memory to make important information visible at a glance.

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Iconic Memory - Why it matters in Design</mark>

Enables users to <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">instantly detect patterns, colours, shapes, and contrast</mark> before conscious attention (pre-attentive processing)  

Supports <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">rapid visual scanning</mark> of interfaces  

Allows important elements to <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">stand out</mark> without overwhelming the user  

Helps users <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">group related elements</mark>  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Motion can attract attention immediately</mark>, guiding the user to key information

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">How Vision is achieved - PRE-ATTENTIVE PROCESSES (ICONIC MEMORY)</mark>
  
Immediate visual impression.  

Subconscious accumulation of information from the environment - Fast, instinctive, efficient  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Gather information from the bottom up:</mark>
- Moving our perception from features to patterns to objects  
- Processed in spatial memory without our conscious action  

Takes less than 500 milliseconds for the eye and the brain to process a pre-attentive property of any image  

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">How Vision is achieved - ATTENTIVE PROCESSES (SHORT TERM MEMORY)</mark>

Focus is directed to relevant elements.  

Brain filters and processes what is important.  

Gather information in a top-down approach - Allowing our perception to move <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">from objects to patterns to features</mark>  

Slow and deliberate

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Cognitive Load</mark>

Mental effort required to process information.  

When cognitive load is too high, users feel overwhelmed, leading to errors, frustration, or disengagement.  

Good design reduces cognitive load by making information easy to scan, process, and act upon.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Types of Cognitive Load:</mark> 
- Intrinsic Load - the complexity of the task itself - (e.g., learning a new language).  
- Extraneous Load - unnecessary distractions due to poor design - (e.g., cluttered UI).  
- Familiarity and Cognitive Load: Users rely on past experiences to process information quickly.  
- Germane Load - the effort that helps users build meaningful knowledge - (e.g., well-structured learning).

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Progressive Disclosure and Cognitive Load</mark> 

Show only necessary details at each step.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> Forms revealing additional fields only when needed (step-by-step wizards).

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Basic principles of visual perception</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Contrast:</mark> Differences in colour, size, shape, or brightness help distinguish elements.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Hierarchy:</mark> Elements are perceived with different levels of importance based on size, placement, or colour.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Balance:</mark> Visual stability through symmetrical or asymmetrical composition.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Alignment:</mark> Positioning elements in a structured way to create order.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Repetition:</mark> Consistency in design elements reinforces recognition and cohesion.

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">ACCENT Framework</mark> 

Framework emphasizes (or accent’s) aspects which determine the effectiveness of a visual display for portraying data.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Consists of six principles:</mark>  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">A - Apprehension:</mark> Ensure clarity and readability.  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">C - Clarity:</mark> Use appropriate visual encodings.  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">C - Consistency:</mark> Maintain uniform design and colour schemes.  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">E - Efficiency:</mark> Avoid unnecessary complexity.  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">N - Necessity:</mark> Focus on essential information.  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">T - Truthfulness:</mark> Represent data accurately.  

The ACCENT principles guide the creation of clear, effective, and ethical visualizations.

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Clarity – Is the encoding appropriate?</mark> 

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Ability to visually distinguish all the elements of a graph:</mark>  
- Avoiding unnecessary clutter and ensuring the visualization is easy to read.  
- Are the most important elements or relations visually most prominent?  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Best Practices:</mark>
- Use readable labels and logical axis scaling.  
- Limit the number of variables in one visualization.  
- Remove distractions (e.g., excessive gridlines, 3D effects).  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> A simplified bar chart with clear labels vs. a chart overloaded with information

![[Pasted image 20260423124712.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Consistency – Is design used consistently?</mark> 

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Ability to interpret a graph based on similarity to previous graphs:</mark>
- Using a uniform design language across visualizations for coherence.  
- Are the elements, symbol shapes and colours consistent with their use in previous graphs?  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Best Practices:</mark>  
- Stick to the same colour schemes and fonts across multiple charts.  
- Ensure axis scales remain consistent when comparing similar data.  
- Use the same visualization types when presenting similar datasets.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> A dashboard where all charts follow the same design style vs. one with mismatched styles.

![[Pasted image 20260423140754.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Efficiency - Is unnecessary complexity removed?</mark> 

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Ability to portray a possibly complex relation in as simple a way as possible:</mark>
- Presenting data in a way that is quick and easy to grasp without unnecessary elements.  
- Are the elements of the graph economically used? Is the graph easy to interpret?  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Best Practices:</mark>
- Use intuitive chart types (e.g., bar charts for comparisons, line charts for trends).  
- Avoid overloading viewers with excessive detail.  
- Use pre-attentive attributes (like colour and position) to highlight key points  
- Example: A heatmap emphasizing high-impact areas vs. a table with too much raw data

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Necessity - Is everything essential?</mark> 

The need for the graph, and the graphical elements.  
- Including only what is essential to support the message.  
- Is the graph a more useful way to represent the data than alternatives (table, text)?  
- Are all the graph elements necessary to convey the relations?  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Best Practices:</mark>  
- Remove non-essential data points or decorations.  
- Ensure every element serves a purpose.  
- Avoid over-labelling or redundant legends.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> A before-and-after comparison of an overloaded chart vs. a refined version.

![[Pasted image 20260423140819.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Truthfulness - Is the data represented honestly?</mark> 

![[Pasted image 20260423140853.png]]

Ability to determine the true value represented by any graphical element by its magnitude relative to the implicit or explicit scale.  
- Are the graph elements accurately positioned and scaled?  
- Ensuring the visualization accurately represents the data without distortion.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Best Practices:</mark>  
- Use appropriate scales (avoid truncating axes misleadingly).  
- Ensure proportions reflect true values (e.g., avoid misleading pie charts).  
- Clearly indicate if data is estimated or incomplete.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> A misleading bar chart with a distorted y-axis vs. a correctly scaled version

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Few’s Best Practices</mark>  

Emphasizes clarity and communication - Useful for creating business dashboards and reports that prioritize clarity.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Emphasize simplicity, accuracy, and meaningful communication in visual design:</mark>  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Simplify:</mark> Remove clutter and unnecessary complexity.  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Emphasize what matters:</mark> Use pre-attentive attributes (colour, size, position) effectively.  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Ensure accuracy:</mark> Avoid distortion and misleading representations.  
- <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Facilitate comparisons:</mark> Make it easy to compare values and trends.  
- Make interactions intuitive

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Simplify</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Remove Clutter and Focus on What Matters:</mark>  
- Every element in a visualization should serve a purpose.  
- Avoid unnecessary gridlines, excessive labels, 3D effects, and decorative elements (chartjunk).  
- Use clean and minimalistic designs to keep attention on the data.  

Everything that doesn’t add meaning is noise. <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> - A simple bar chart without excessive colours or gridlines is more effective including lots of visual elements that don’t contribute

![[Pasted image 20260423141458.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Emphasize What Matters</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Guide Attention Effectively:</mark>  
- Use pre-attentive attributes like colour, size, position, and contrast to highlight important information.  
- Ensure the key message is immediately visible without unnecessary effort.  
- Place data elements in a way that follows natural reading patterns (left-to-right, top-to-bottom).  

If everything is highlighted, nothing is.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> Use colour to direct the audience’s attention immediately to the key message

![[Pasted image 20260423141557.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Ensure Accuracy:</mark> 

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Avoid Distortions:</mark>
- Visualizations must preserve the integrity of the data.  
- Avoid misleading axes, improper scaling, or cherry- picking data ranges that exaggerate trends.  
- Use consistent intervals on axes and avoid truncated bars or stretched line charts.  

Design choices can distort truth.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> A bar chart where the Y-axis extends to 40 rather than a more realistic value can falsely minimise differences between values.

![[Pasted image 20260423141654.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Facilitate Comparisons</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Make it Easy to See Patterns:</mark> 
- Comparisons should be clear and intuitive.  
- Use side-by-side charts instead of separate images spread across multiple slides or pages.
- When appropriate, use small multiples (a grid of small charts using the same scale) to show different categories.  

If users can’t compare easily, the chart has failed.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> A series of smaller charts can work better than one big cluttered chart.

![[Pasted image 20260423141808.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Design for Meaningful Interactivity</mark>  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Dashboards and interactive reports should respond dynamically to user inputs:</mark> 
- Use filters, tooltips, and drill-downs to provide more details without overwhelming the main display. 
- Ensure smooth navigation between different levels of detail.  

Interaction should reveal more — not add confusion.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Example:</mark> A business dashboard allows users to click on a <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">geographic region</mark> to view specific sales figures for that region.

![[Pasted image 20260423141908.png]]

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Few’s three key questions to ask:</mark> 

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Does the visualization tell the right story?</mark> The design should align with the intended message.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Can the audience interpret it correctly in seconds?</mark> If it takes too long to understand, the visualization needs simplification.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Does it avoid unnecessary complexity?</mark> Avoid clutter, 3D effects, and unnecessary embellishments.
