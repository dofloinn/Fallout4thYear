### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">How much data is being generated?</mark>  

Giorgia Lupi and Stefanie Posavec:  

"Besides finding data in the world around us, we are all creating data just by living: our purchases, our movements through the city, our explorations across the internet, all contribute to the 'data trail' we leave in our walk as we move through life.“  

Posavec, S., & Lupi, G. (2016). Dear Data. Particular Books.

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Total Global Data Created (2025):</mark> Estimated at ~230 zettabytes (ZB) of data globally by the end of 2025  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Daily Data Creation:</mark> Around 550–600 ZB of new data created per year, which translates to roughly 1.5–1.6 ZB per day  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Value of Data Economy:</mark> Some forecasts estimate the data economy contribution to reach up to $400+ billion annually by 2025, depending on how “value of data” is defined (data services, analytics, cloud, storage, etc.).  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Connected IoT Devices:</mark> Global IoT devices are projected to exceed 64 billion+ by 2025, up from previous estimates of ~55.7 billion.  

These devices are expected to generate ~79–90 ZB of data cumulatively by 2025.  

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Unstructured Data Share:</mark> Multiple industry surveys (Gartner, IDC, Veritas) estimate ~80–90% of enterprise data is unstructured (text, audio, video, logs, social activity, etc.).

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Statistics Example</mark>

It is possible to generate bivariate data with a given mean, median, and correlation in any shape you like — even a dinosaur:  

The datasauraus dozen: https://www.research.autodesk.com/publications/same-stats-different-graphs/  

Contains different datasets, each has the same summary statistics (mean, std. deviation, and Pearson’s corr.) to 2 decimal places. (x͞=54.02, y͞ = 48.09, sdx = 14.52, sdy = 24.79, Pearson’s r = +0.32)  

Matejka, J., & Fitzmaurice, G. (2017, May). Same stats, different graphs: generating datasets with varied appearance and identical statistics through simulated annealing. In Proceedings of the 2017 CHI conference on human factors in computing systems (pp. 1290-1294).  
  
Code to generate: L1-Datasaurus.r

Each of these has the same mean, standard deviation and (Pearson) correlation.

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Why Visualisation?</mark>

Mattson, M. P. (2014). Superior pattern processing is the essence of the evolved human brain. Frontiers in neuroscience, 8, 265.  

![[Pasted image 20260128162422.png]]

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Superior pattern processing (SPP):</mark>
- Fundamental basis of most, if not all, unique features of the human brain including intelligence, language, imagination, invention, and the belief in imaginary entities such as ghosts and gods.  
- Involves the electrochemical, neuronal network-based, encoding, integration, and transfer to other individuals of perceived or mentally-fabricated patterns.

During human evolution, pattern processing capabilities became increasingly sophisticated as the result of expansion of the cerebral cortex  

Particularly the prefrontal cortex and regions involved in processing of images  

Specific patterns, real or imagined, are reinforced by emotional experiences, indoctrination and even psychedelic drugs.  

Impaired or dysregulated SPP is fundamental to cognitive and psychiatric disorders.

### <mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">What makes a great visualisation?</mark>

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">They are:</mark>
- Truthful
- Functional
- Beautiful  
- Insightful  
- Enlightening

Cairo, A. (2016). The truthful art: Data, charts, and maps for communication. New Riders Publishing.
 
Graphical excellence is the well-designed presentation of interesting data—a matter of substance, of statistics, and of design ...  
- It consists of complex ideas communicated with clarity, precision, and efficiency. ...
- It is that which gives to the viewer the greatest number of ideas in the shortest time with the least ink in the smallest space ...
- It is nearly always multivariate ...
- And graphical excellence requires telling the truth about the data.“  
- Tufte, E. R. (1985). The visual display of quantitative information. The Journal for Healthcare Quality (JHQ), 7(3), 15

<mark style="background: #04FF00A6;">Great visualisation features:</mark>
- Good aesthetics
- No substantive issues
- No perceptual issues
- Honesty + good judgment  

Healy, K. (2018). Data visualization: a practical introduction. Princeton University Press.
