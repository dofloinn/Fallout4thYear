### <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">How to Research Effectively:</mark>

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Choices:</mark>
- What problem will I solve?  
- How should I solve it?  
- What programming language should I use?  
- What hardware should I use?  
- How should I design it?  
- Where do I even start?

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">The role of research:</mark>
- Research gives us a framework for making informed choices  
- Research forces us to move outside of our comfort zone and evaluate alternatives  
- Research saves us from re-inventing the wheel

### <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Larry the bulk-and-cut llama:</mark>

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Research starts with bulking:</mark>
- Option discovery  
- Low quality control  
- Focus on breadth  
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">End-product:</mark> options  

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Research then moves to cutting:</mark>
- Find a focus  
- High quality control  
- Focus on depth  
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">End-product:</mark> plan

### <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Phase 1 - bulk</mark>

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">bulk:</mark>
- What is the current problem you are trying to solve?  
- How are people trying to solve it now?  
- Aim for 3 potential avenues

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Resources:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Wikipedia:</mark> excellent resource (particularly at the beginning). Never cite it!
- Literature Reviews
- Past Projects

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Approach:</mark>
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Quick scan:</mark> forget the details, you can come back later  
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Peer-reviewed papers:</mark> Abstract, Intro, Conclusion
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Past Projects:</mark> Chapter 2, Results, Conclusions  
- Add everything to your citation manager

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Questions to Answer:</mark>
- What problem is being solved?  
- How did they solve it?  
- What were the results? Accuracy, Self-reported benefits/drawbacks, Performance metrics (if included)  
- Any identified future work?

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Wrap-Up:</mark>
- Write out your list of options  
- Choose the option that looks most fun / interesting / likely to succeed  
- Keep a back-up option in mind  
- Start cutting!

### <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Phase 2: Cut</mark>

Focus on the papers that match your chosen approach  

Move any irrelevant papers out of the way

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Approach:</mark>
- Pick one or two papers which look promising
- Try to understand what’s being done in that paper  
- Write out a bullet point summary

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Wrap-Up:</mark>
- Go back to Phase 1 
- This time, focus on your chosen approach

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Citation Crawling:</mark>  
- Academic papers have a list of citations (older papers only)  
- Google scholar lets you search papers which cited a particular paper (newer papers)  
- Use these to find more sources

### <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Using a Reference Manager</mark>

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Why Use a Reference Manager?</mark>
- Keeping track of references is difficult  
- Formatting references correctly is difficult  
- Reference managers let you add your own notes to papers you found

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Zotero:</mark> 
- Link in Brightspace on how to install  
- Includes a browser plug-in to quickly add papers  
- Includes a Word plugin to generate bibliographies
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Using the Web Plugin:</mark> Add references to your library directly from your browser

### <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Overview of Chapter 2</mark>

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">What makes a good Chapter 2?</mark>
- Tells an engaging story of the development of the relevant technology  
- Contains many good quality up-to-date citations  
- Engages with the literature  
- Shows how the research has informed project choices

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Introduction:</mark>
- Short section  
- Signposts telling the reader what’s coming up in each following sub-section  
- In this chapter...

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Alternative Existing Solutions:</mark> 
- Tells the story of the problem  
- When did it first become of interest  
- What were the early approaches  
- What new difficulties have come up  
- What are the current approaches

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Alternative Approaches:</mark> 
- Sub-sub-section for each approach  
- Brief overview of approach  
- Under what circumstances does it work well  
- Under what circumstances does it not work so well  
- Why is it (not) suitable for your project

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Technologies:</mark>
- Break your project down based on technologies used (client / server / database etc.)  
- For each one outline 2 – 3 options  
- Keep these brief and focussed on your project  
- State your justification and choice for each technology  
- Beware of marketing hype and fan-boys!

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Other Research:</mark>
- Requirements research  
- Research on evaluation metrics  
- Anything else you researched for the FYP

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Existing FYPs:</mark>
- Do include this  
- You may not (hopefully will not find an exact match)  
- Be creative in how you link them  
- Similar problems  
- Similar technology

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Conclusions:</mark> 
- Summarise the chapter  
- Big takeaways from alternative approaches (how they feed into your project)  
- Technologies chosen  
- Big takeaways from other research  
- Lessons learned from existing FYPs (if applicable)

<mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Chapter 2 Checklist:</mark>
- Do you have a wide variety of citations (aim for 30)  
- Are your citations generally high quality (peer reviewed, ideally)  
- Are you including up-to-date references (last 5 years)
- Do you show how the research has informed your decisions?
